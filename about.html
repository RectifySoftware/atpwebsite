About the Series

A ten-day holiday. A five-day eye-contact streak. One unanswered question.

Across the Pool is an LGBTQ+ coming-of-age romantic comedy and drama, told in close to real time, about an 18-year-old named Sam who develops a crush on a boy at his resort in Menorca, Spain, and never quite works up the nerve to say hello.

The Premise

Sam is on a ten-day family holiday in Menorca. On day two, he notices a boy at the resort playing on an inflatable with his younger siblings, tall, blonde, blue-eyed, probably around Sam's age. Sam never learns his name, so he gives him one anyway: Surfer.

What follows is days of eye contact, near-misses, one very memorable incident involving a t-shirt, and a nod on the lobby stairs that Sam immediately overanalyses. Nothing has been said out loud. Everything has been felt intensely. With three days of the holiday left, the question hanging over the whole series is simple: what if the looks actually mean something?

Themes

First Crushes

The specific chaos of liking someone you've never spoken to.

Overthinking

Turning one glance into a four-act emotional narrative.

Miscommunication

Two people, zero conversations, endless meaning assigned anyway.

Holiday Romance

Ten days, a ticking clock, and a resort pool doing a lot of narrative heavy lifting.

Setting

A family-friendly resort on the island of Menorca, Spain, built around a pool, a stretch of coastline, and the kind of lobby staircase where entire romantic subplots apparently take place at 10:30pm.

Tone

Warm, funny, romantic, awkward, summery, and occasionally bittersweet. Never relentlessly dramatic. The comedy comes from Sam's self-awareness, and from the gap between the version of him trying to act normal by the pool and the version of him internally losing his mind about a nod.

A Note

This story is still unfolding

Across the Pool is being told in close to real time, as the actual ten-day holiday plays out. Nothing about how it ends has been decided or written in advance, which means Surfer's feelings, his sexuality, his real name and even his nationality (Irish is highly likely, going by his dad's accent, but it's not confirmed) stay genuinely open. Sam's interpretations, throughout, are Sam's interpretations, not facts. LGBTQ+ identity here is part of the story, not a theme layered on top of it.
